# Every documented framework gap affecting us, and the workaround for each

**Scope:** every gap, bug, undocumented behaviour, or missing capability found across `sdk.md`, `staking.md`, and `payments.md`, plus a few found only by cross-referencing Scoresheet's own `SPEC.md`/`BRIEF.md` against the protocol docs, gathered into one list with a workaround for each. Ordered roughly by how directly it bears on the two features being built (a puzzle-pool stake, a player-staked tournament). Every row carries a source; nothing here is invented.

## 2. Why it works

Most of these are not carelessness — they are consequences of two deliberate design choices that are individually sound but compound into real gaps when combined. First, **the provider is a thin, minimal message-passing shim** (`sdk.md` §2): it exposes exactly ten host-mediated methods and nothing else, which is why there is no `getBalance`, no `getStaker`, and no HTLC method — the provider's surface is intentionally the smallest thing that lets Nimiq Pay mediate every sensitive action, not a general-purpose chain-query API. Second, **the staking protocol deliberately keeps on-chain bookkeeping at validator scale**, pushing reward fan-out to stakers entirely off-chain (`staking.md` §2) — a real, reasoned trade-off for keeping the chain cheap, not an oversight, but one that leaves a genuine, undocumented risk for anyone who assumes it works like a normal yield-bearing account.

## 3. What they do badly

The worst pattern across this whole list is **documentation silence rather than documentation error**: most of these (§1.6/#9, the HTLC absence/#8, the reporting-window reset/#19, the confirmation instability/#13) are not wrong in the docs — they are simply **absent from the Mini Apps documentation tree specifically**, discoverable only by reading the separate `/protocol/` pages, a community call transcript, or by executing code against the real library. A builder who reads only `nimiq.dev/mini-apps` — which is the officially recommended path (§10 of `NIMIQ_DEV_DOCS_FULL_REFERENCE.md`, the AI skill's own install) — would encounter none of #2, #8, #9, #10, #15, #18, or #19 before shipping.

## 4. What we should copy conceptually

Scoresheet's own existing defensive patterns (`wallet.ts:unwrap()`, `withTimeout()`, `memoFits()`, `staking.ts`'s recorded-principal approach, `pool.ts`'s hard-ceiling `budgetFor()`) already correctly work around gaps #1, #3, #4, and partially #9 — the right habit going forward is to extend the *same* patterns to every new staking call rather than writing new one-off guards, and to keep treating "the docs don't mention it" as a reason to check the protocol/source tree directly rather than as evidence it doesn't exist.

## 5. What we can do better

The two gaps with no existing workaround at all today are #2 (staking-read layer) and #14 (settlement watcher) — both are server-side RPC work, both follow patterns Scoresheet has already half-built elsewhere (`stake-cli.ts`'s balance/staker readers; `nimiq-payout.ts`'s broadcast path), and both are prerequisites for building feature (b) honestly: a tournament that can't show a player their real stake state, or can't independently confirm a stake actually settled, is building on the same confirmation-instability problem (#13) it should be defending against.

## 6. What is technically required

Closing #2 and #14 needs: a long-lived server-side RPC/light-client connection (already the pattern in `stake-cli.ts`), the two additional method calls (`getStakerByAddress` full-field read, `getValidatorByAddress`), and — for #14 specifically — either `addTransactionListener`/`getTransactionsByAddress` on a `@nimiq/core` light client (verified working server-side in Node, `verification/01` §6.1) or a second independent RPC endpoint for reconciliation, per the architecture `verification/01` §6.3 already recommends and that nothing in the codebase implements yet.

## 7. What could break

If #9/#10 are not designed around explicitly, the most likely real-world failure is a quiet one: the pool's `earnedLuna` simply never grows because the chosen validator doesn't forward rewards on any usable schedule, and nobody notices until someone asks "why hasn't the pool paid out anything in three weeks." If #16 is not respected, a "smooth" tournament-entry UX that tries to chain staking calls without individual taps will simply not work against a real Nimiq Pay build — this is a documented anti-pattern, not a style preference. If #13/#14 are ignored, players will see false "still pending" states for money that has already moved, and will reasonably conclude the app is broken.

## 8. What we can uniquely do because of Nimiq

Despite the list above, the honest net position is strong: #10's constraint — no seizure, ever, without the owner's signature — is exactly the property that makes the epoch-boundary cooldown (`staking.md` §8) a genuinely novel, zero-custody commitment mechanic once it's designed around rather than against. Nobody in the reviewed catalog has built anything on the staking surface at all (`sdk.md` §5), so even the *workarounds* in this file — a self-built staking-read layer, a settlement watcher, a validator chosen for its transparent payout policy — would themselves be a real, checkable differentiator, not just risk mitigation.
