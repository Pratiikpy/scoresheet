# nnue-pytorch — how Stockfish's evaluation is trained

Research date: 2026-09-08. Sources: https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch (WebFetch), https://raw.githubusercontent.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/master/docs/nnue.md (WebFetch, full raw file), https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/wiki/Training-datasets (WebFetch), https://raw.githubusercontent.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/master/LICENSE (WebFetch), https://github.com/official-stockfish/networks (WebFetch), and targeted WebSearch queries, as cited inline. Read alongside `stockfish.md` (which covers the engine that consumes these networks) and against this project's own prior, already-decided history: it ported the akimbo NNUE (MIT) exactly, verified it was a stronger player, and rejected it because it made game review worse.

## 1. What they do

- `nnue-pytorch` is the official PyTorch training framework for Stockfish's **NNUE** ("Efficiently Updatable Neural Network," an architecture invented by Yu Nasu for Shogi and adapted for chess by Hisayori Noda) evaluation networks (source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/master/docs/nnue.md, direct WebFetch of the full doc).
- **Architecture**: a large, extremely sparse **feature transformer** (the first layer) followed by a small number of dense layers — typical shapes cited in the doc are `HalfKP[40960]->256->32->32->1` or newer `1024x2->8->32->1`-style configurations. Feature counts run into the tens of thousands (**82,672** cited for the SFNNv13-era `HalfKAv2_hm` feature set per WebSearch of chessprogramming.org and DeepWiki summaries), but only a tiny fraction (~0.1% density) are active for any given position (source: nnue.md, direct WebFetch).
- **The accumulator**: instead of a full forward pass through the sparse first layer on every position, NNUE maintains a cached "accumulator" — for each side (white/black perspective), a vector of 16-bit ints equal to the sum of the weight columns for all currently-active features plus a bias vector. A move only changes a handful of features, so the accumulator is updated incrementally: subtract the weight column for a feature that became inactive (e.g. a captured piece), add the column for one that became active — never recomputed from scratch (source: nnue.md, direct WebFetch; corroborated by chessprogramming.org's Stockfish NNUE page via WebSearch).
- **Feature sets**: `HalfKP` encodes `(our_king_square, piece_square, piece_type, piece_color)` tuples (64×64×5×2 = 40,960 features); newer sets like `HalfKAv2` also encode king position on both sides; `Full_Threats` is a specialized threat-aware set (source: nnue.md, direct WebFetch). Stockfish 19's SFNNv16 specifically "eliminat[ed] redundant threat features while adding new pawn-pair features" (source: `stockfish.md` §1, from the SF19 blog post).
- **Quantization**: training happens in floating point, but inference must run in integer arithmetic for CPU speed. The doc states the target is "million(s) of evaluations per second per thread," which "requires extreme solutions, and most importantly quantization." Feature-transformer weights/accumulator are int16, scaled by 127 to match a `ClippedReLU` activation range of [0, 127]; dense-layer weights are int8, scaled by 64, with accumulation in int32; a `Quantmoid4` piecewise function approximates a sigmoid for the final output entirely in integer arithmetic (source: nnue.md, direct WebFetch, full detail retained). Stockfish 19 specifically trained using **Quantization-Aware Training (QAT)**, i.e. the quantization error is modeled *during* training rather than only applied afterward (source: `stockfish.md` §1, SF19 blog post).
- **Training loss**: MSE or cross-entropy, computed in "WDL-space" (0 = loss, 0.5 = draw, 1 = win) by passing the centipawn evaluation through a sigmoid with a scaling factor (Stockfish's own doc cites ~410), and interpolated between "was this evaluation accurate" and "did the side that this position favored actually win the game" via a lambda parameter (source: nnue.md, direct WebFetch).
- **Feature factorization during training**: "virtual" features (P-factors generalizing across king positions, K-factors for king position, HalfRelativeKP-factors for piece-relative-to-king position) exist only during training to speed convergence, then get folded/coalesced into the real feature weights before the network is exported for inference (source: nnue.md, direct WebFetch).
- **Training data**: generated by self-play — Stockfish itself plays games at a fixed shallow search depth (an official example dataset used **16 billion positions at depth 9**, with `random_multi_pv 4` / `random_multi_pv_diff 100` to add opening diversity), output in the project's own **binpack** format. The currently-recommended official mixed dataset, `T60T70wIsRightFarseer.binpack`, blends Lc0's T60/T70 self-play data, Stockfish's own self-play data specifically from "openings it usually gets wrong," and further converted Lc0 data. The documented best practice is to first train on Stockfish-generated data (depth 9, ~5000 nodes), then *retrain* an already-decent network on Lc0-derived data, because "high quality datasets like Leela Chess Zero achieve better results when used to retrain a network trained on lower quality datasets... than when training on the high quality datasets from zero" (source: https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/wiki/Training-datasets, direct WebFetch). Stockfish 19's own SFNNv16 specifically used "hundreds of billions of positions rescored using a strong Leela net" (source: `stockfish.md` §1).
- **Tooling**: `train.py` (full control) and `easy_train.py` (simplified), with `ddp_launcher.py` for distributed multi-GPU training, TensorBoard logging, Docker images for NVIDIA/AMD GPUs and Apple Silicon MPS, and a `run_games.py` utility to play-test a freshly trained network (source: https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch README, direct WebFetch).
- **SIMD-optimized inference**: AVX2 code processes 16 int16 values per register in the feature transformer; dense layers use 4×4-unrolled int8×int8→int32 dot-product instructions; a sparse-input path activates when hidden-layer density drops to ~10–15%, skipping zero inputs entirely via index extraction and lookup tables (source: nnue.md, direct WebFetch).
- Architecture naming has evolved from **SFNNv1 through SFNNv16** (Stockfish 19's network), broadly trending toward larger feature-transformer widths (256 → 1024 neurons cited) while keeping the deeper layers small (source: nnue.md + `stockfish.md` §1).

## 2. Why it works

- **Sparsity + incrementality is the whole trick.** Because only ~0.1% of tens of thousands of features are ever active, and a chess move changes only a few of them, the expensive part of a neural net (the huge first layer) is never actually recomputed from scratch during search — only a handful of vector add/subtract operations per move, an update cost closer to a hand-written incremental eval term than to a real forward pass.
- **Quantization exploits integer SIMD throughput** that floating-point inference cannot match on commodity CPUs, and NNUE's shallow depth means "quantization error is negligible" (source: nnue.md) — the accuracy cost of going to int8/int16 is small precisely because there are few layers for the error to compound across.
- **Concentrating knowledge in the first layer, and keeping everything after it small, is a deliberate, stated design choice**, not an accident: the doc states "after that first layer the network needs to sharply reduce its width" and that "adding further small layers near the output adds little to accuracy, and may even be harmful if quantization is employed due to error accumulation" (source: nnue.md, direct WebFetch, quoted from the earlier fetch pass on this same file). This is the architectural reason NNUE nets stay fast enough for a search that visits millions of nodes per second.
- **Training-data curation (mixing self-play with Lc0-derived data, retraining rather than training from scratch on the highest-quality data) is itself a tuned, iteratively-discovered process** — the wiki's own stated best-practice order (Stockfish data first, Lc0 retrain second) reflects years of community experimentation, not a first-principles derivation.

## 3. What they do badly

- **The net is uninterpretable, and this is the exact, specific failure mode our own project already encountered.** Nothing in the architecture — feature transformer weights, accumulator state, quantized activations — decomposes into a human-legible "why" for a given score. Per project background: we ported akimbo's NNUE (MIT), verified it was a stronger player, and **rejected it specifically because it made game review worse**, despite the Elo gain. This document's own read of the architecture confirms why that outcome should have been expected: NNUE was never designed to be explainable, only fast and accurate in aggregate — there is no path from "the network output −0.34" to "because your knight is undefended and your king is exposed on the g-file" without building an entirely separate explanation layer on top, at which point the network's own reasoning is bypassed anyway.
- **Training data provenance is genuinely ad hoc**, not a clean, documented spec: the current recommended dataset is a hand-assembled mixture of Stockfish self-play (biased toward "openings it usually gets wrong," itself a moving, informally curated target), converted Lc0 T60/T70 data, and "some more converted Lc0 data" (source: Training-datasets wiki page, direct WebFetch, quoted verbatim). This is tribal knowledge accumulated on a wiki, not a versioned, reproducible dataset specification — reproducing a specific historical Stockfish network's exact training data from scratch would be difficult even with full access to the same tooling.
- **The infrastructure bar is high**: the official example dataset alone is 16 billion positions; official training uses multi-GPU distributed training (`ddp_launcher.py`) and dedicated Docker images. This is out of reach for casual iteration and is exactly the kind of ongoing infrastructure commitment a small team should weigh carefully before re-adopting NNUE for any reason.
- **A trained network is only as trustworthy as the SPRT test that validates it** (see `fishtest.md`) — training/validation loss decreasing is explicitly *not* sufficient evidence of a real improvement in play; nnue-pytorch's own `run_games.py` exists precisely because loss curves alone are known to be unreliable predictors of actual strength gain, mirroring the exact caution fishtest's own FAQ raises about biased Elo estimates (see `fishtest.md` §3).

## 4. What we should copy conceptually

- **The accumulator/incremental-update pattern generalizes far beyond neural nets.** Any per-move state we compute that changes only slightly between adjacent positions (e.g., incremental material count, incremental piece-square-table sum, incremental king-safety/attacker counts, incremental pawn-structure hash) benefits from the same idea: update the few cells that changed rather than recomputing the whole term from scratch on every node. If our engine's evaluation function has any O(board-size) term recomputed at every leaf, this is the concrete, proven pattern for making it O(changed squares) instead — without needing a neural net to justify it.
- **Never trust a training-time metric (loss) as proof of a real improvement — validate with actual games, gated the same way any other change is gated** (the `run_games.py` step, tied directly into `fishtest.md`'s SPRT discipline). A "the eval got more accurate on held-out positions" result is the neural-net equivalent of "it won 7/10 games" if it isn't backed by a proper significance test against real play.
- **Retraining on a better-but-different-style data source, rather than training from scratch on it, can beat training from scratch** — the Stockfish→Lc0-retrain finding (§1) is a specific, evidenced instance of a broader idea (warm-starting from a decent baseline rather than starting from nothing) that could apply anywhere we're tuning parameters/weights from data, not just for a neural net.

## 5. What we can do better

- **We can build (or keep) an evaluation whose terms are named and explainable, because our product's success metric is game-review quality, not raw Elo — and we have already made this call once, explicitly, by rejecting a stronger NNUE for exactly this reason.** A classical-style eval (material, king safety, pawn structure, mobility, as named terms) that can attach a natural-language explanation to *why* it scored a position the way it did is not a lesser version of NNUE — it is optimizing for a different, and for us more important, axis that neither Stockfish's SFNNv1–16 lineage nor lc0's transformer network has ever targeted, because neither project ships a teaching product.
- **We can go further than pre-NNUE classical Stockfish ever did**, because that classical eval, even when it existed, was never built or exposed as a teaching tool — it was purely an internal scoring function competing for Elo against every other candidate eval, same as NNUE replaced it for. We can deliberately attach templated, learner-facing language to each eval term ("your queen is undefended," "this pawn structure weakens f6") specifically for amateur-player game review, which is a genuinely new axis of value neither reference project has built.
- **If we ever do want a data-driven quality signal, it should be trained on and validated against review-quality labels (does a human agree this was a good explanation), not just win/draw/loss outcomes** — see §8 for how Nimiq could fund exactly that labeled dataset, which is the one thing this whole research area (Stockfish/fishtest/nnue-pytorch/lc0) has never needed to build, because none of them optimize for explanation quality.

## 6. What is technically required

- To train an NNUE-class network at all: a large labeled position dataset (order of billions of positions for anything close to Stockfish's own scale — clearly infeasible for us to replicate at that scale), a GPU (or many-core CPU) training pipeline in PyTorch, a custom quantization-aware training loop matched exactly to the target inference arithmetic, and inference code (in our case, TypeScript/WASM rather than Stockfish's native C++/AVX2) that reproduces the exact quantized arithmetic used at training time.
- **Any mismatch between the quantization scheme used at training time and the one actually implemented at inference time produces a silently weaker network** — no crash, no error, just measurably worse play that would need the same fishtest-style SPRT discipline described in `fishtest.md` to even detect, because loss-curve numbers alone would look fine.
- None of this is required for the eval direction recommended in §5 (a legible, hand-authored eval with named terms) — that path needs domain knowledge and iteration, not GPU infrastructure, and is consistent with what our project has already built and already chosen to keep over a neural alternative.

## 7. What could break

- **WASM/JS integer SIMD is materially weaker than native AVX2/AVX-VNNI for exactly the operations NNUE inference depends on.** The doc's own performance section (§1) describes int8×int8→int32 dot-product instructions (`_mm256_dpbusd_epi32`-class AVX2 intrinsics) as central to fast dense-layer inference; WebAssembly's SIMD128 proposal exists but its int8 dot-product support is narrower and generally slower than native AVX2/AVX-VNNI, meaning a hypothetical NNUE eval ported to our TS/WASM engine would cost proportionally more time per node than it costs native Stockfish, shrinking the effective search depth reachable in the same time budget on a mobile WebView — compounding, not just matching, the CPU disadvantage a mobile device already has versus a desktop Stockfish benchmark. **This performance-ratio claim about WASM SIMD128 vs. native AVX2/AVX-VNNI for int8 dot products is standard, well-documented WebAssembly-vs-native behavior but was not independently benchmarked in this research pass — flag as NOT VERIFIED at the specific-numbers level, verified only at the qualitative "WASM SIMD is narrower than native AVX-VNNI" level via the architecture described in nnue.md plus general WebAssembly SIMD proposal scope.**
- **Quantization/training mismatches are silent by nature** (§6) — this is the same class of risk that likely contributed to, or at minimum would be indistinguishable from, whatever specifically made the ported akimbo NNUE worse for review even while testing stronger in raw play: a subtly different numeric behavior between training-time and inference-time arithmetic degrades some property of the output (possibly review-relevant stability or move preference) without breaking the top-line Elo metric that was actually being measured.
- **Any renewed attempt at a neural eval reopens exactly the decision already made and closed** (ported, verified, rejected) — the risk here isn't technical, it's process: re-litigating a settled product decision without new evidence specific to the review-quality axis (not just a new, stronger network) would be repeating work already done.

## 8. What we can uniquely do because of Nimiq

- **The single most valuable, genuinely novel thing Nimiq enables here is funding a review-quality-labeled dataset that no existing chess-engine project has ever needed to build.** Stockfish's training data (self-play + Lc0 conversion, per §1) and Lc0's own training data are both optimized purely for "did the side favored by this evaluation actually win" — an outcome label, cheap to generate automatically via self-play, needing no human judgment at all. Our product needs a fundamentally different label: **is this explanation of a flagged position actually useful to a human learner** — a qualitative judgment that self-play cannot generate no matter how many games are played.
- Nimiq's feeless, instant NIM micropayments make it economically viable to pay real users a small bounty per position they annotate (e.g., "why is this a blunder," rated on a real game the player themselves played through our app) — a proprietary, review-quality-labeled dataset, funded at a per-label cost that would be uneconomical over a traditional payment rail (card-processor minimums, multi-day payout cycles) but is exactly the shape of transaction Nimiq is built for. This directly extends the idea introduced in `fishtest.md` §8 (paying contributors for verified test batches) into the training-data domain, which is where it actually has the most product value, since it targets the precise metric (§5's review-quality axis) that made us reject NNUE in the first place rather than trying to out-Elo Stockfish on its own terms.
